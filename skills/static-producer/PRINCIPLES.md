# Static ad principles (Meta, DTC)

A short list of rules, each with its source. Where the evidence is thin or comes from a vendor,
it says so. The numbers are the sources' own; we haven't re-tested them. Researched Nov 2026 via web
search. motionapp.com and alexcooper.beehiiv.com could not be fetched from this sandbox, so
anything taken from them is based on search-result summaries and should be checked against the
original before you quote it to a client.

## 1. One message, scannable in a second

- **Keep it simple.** Alex Cooper (Adcrate) lists "Keep it simple stupid" as a rule for
  converting statics and warns against being too clever. ([7 Rules for High Converting Static Ads](https://alexcooper.beehiiv.com/p/7-rules-for-high-converting-static-ads), Feb 2025)
- **Statics have hooks too.** Cooper's point is that people don't read a static from top to
  bottom. The headline and visual are the hook, so design for instant scanning. (same source)
- **One benefit, one proof point, one CTA.** This is the consensus in practitioner guides. Keep
  on-image text short. ([Segwise, anatomy of a static ad](https://segwise.ai/blog/anatomy-high-converting-static-ad), [Hawky](https://hawky.ai/blog/static-ads-that-convert))
- In this kit: one headline (best under ~8 words), one supporting element (chart, review,
  callouts or price) and one CTA. Auto-fit shrinks type when copy runs long. Treat that as a sign
  to cut copy, not as the fix.

## 2. Build for cold traffic first

- Cooper: warm audiences will convert from good ads made for cold audiences, but cold audiences
  won't convert from ads made for warm audiences. Write headlines a stranger understands with no
  brand context. ([source](https://alexcooper.beehiiv.com/p/7-rules-for-high-converting-static-ads))
- Use the customer's words. Practitioner guides report that headlines in customer language beat
  marketing language. Mine reviews, Reddit and support tickets for phrasing (see Adlicio /
  voice-of-customer tools). ([Segwise](https://segwise.ai/blog/anatomy-high-converting-static-ad))

## 3. Work from proven formats, then mash them up

- Motion's write-up of Obvi's static playbook, which Motion credits with helping Obvi reach
  $40M in sales, lists 8 formats: Lifestyle, UGC, Render, Review, Community, Us vs Them,
  Problem vs Solution, News/Media. ([Motion talk, Feb 2024](https://motionapp.com/library/talk/2024-ad-creative-secrets-from-8-figure-dtc-brands-feat-obvi/))
- Ad-library boards that practitioners share group statics as "Us vs Them", "Hard Promise",
  "Benefit Call-Out" and "Announcements". ([Master Static Ad Repository](https://therohitkumar.substack.com/p/master-static-ad-repository), Atria boards)
- Cooper said in a 2024 Motion talk that "85% of our winning ads in 2023 were mashups":
  proven formats combined with a new angle. ([Motion](https://motionapp.com/blog/ai-ad-creative-alex-cooper))
- Template libraries like Creative OS (800+ Figma templates, used by Adcrate) are where
  layouts come from. This kit's seven layouts cover the most common families. ([Creative OS](https://www.creativeos.com/creativecon-2025/how-i-make-thousands-of-high-converting-ad-creatives-every-month/))
- Long-running competitor ads are a signal: a static that has run for 60+ days in the Meta Ad
  Library is probably converting. This is a heuristic, not proof. ([Segwise](https://segwise.ai/blog/anatomy-high-converting-static-ad))

## 4. Native beats polished, sometimes

- Barry Hott's "ugly ads" idea: less polished content that looks native to the platform
  competes better with organic posts. "Ugly" doesn't mean low quality, and opening on a logo
  hurts. ([Banknotes podcast](https://banknotes-audio.simplecast.com/episodes/ugly-ads-dont-mean-bad-ads-try-these-expert-tips-for-high-intent-ads-U4cDM19V), [Cooper: 7 lessons from Barry Hott](https://alexcooper.beehiiv.com/p/7-facebook-advertising-lessons-i-learned-from-barry-hott))
- In this kit, the `native` layout (phone note / tweet / text post) has no CTA button, and the
  brand mark is either missing or shown as a regular account name. Test it alongside the polished
  layouts.

## 5. Diversity over volume

- Motion recommends mapping your creative mix (static vs video, lo-fi vs polished, emotional vs
  rational) to find gaps. Don't ship 20 variants of one idea. ([Motion, static ads creative strategy](https://motionapp.com/blog/static-ads-creative-strategy))
- Cooper's ideation funnel (Motion webinar): research (your ideas, AI ideas, outside
  inspiration), then a braindump of about 10 ideas, then about 4 concepts into production.
  ([Motion talk](https://motionapp.com/library/talk/building-winning-ads-live-feat-alex-cooper/))
- Use statics as cheap tests of angles, hooks and offers. Practitioners often move a proven
  static angle into motion/video afterwards. ([Realize](https://realize.com/marketing-hub/digital-brothers-enhance-performance-motion-ads/), vendor source)
- In this kit: vary **angle and layout** across a batch. Use headline variants (a/b/c) for
  within-concept tests, one variable at a time.

## 6. Read results correctly

- Cooper: hook rate and hold rate are diagnostics, not success metrics. Judge on
  CPA/ROAS. ([Motion](https://motionapp.com/blog/ai-ad-creative-alex-cooper))
- Motion's "Top Performing" report tracks rising, flat and declining creatives to catch fatigue
  early. ([Motion help](https://help.motionapp.com/en/articles/8757736-find-emerging-creative-winners))

## 7. Placement mechanics

- Feed 4:5 (1080x1350) is the default static; 1:1 (1080x1080) covers carousels, which crop
  cards to 1:1, and older placements. ([adsuploader](https://adsuploader.com/blog/meta-ads-aspect-ratios))
- Safe zones: third-party guides citing Meta's Ads Guide give roughly 14% top / 35% bottom /
  6% sides for Reels and about 20% bottom for Stories. Feed margins vary by source. This kit keeps
  key text out of the top and bottom 14% on 4:5. Check final placements in Ads Manager's preview.
  ([Billo](https://billo.app/blog/meta-ads-safe-zones), [adkit safe zones](https://adkit.so/tools/safe-zones/meta))

## About "Alex Cooper"

The famous Alex Cooper is the *Call Her Daddy* podcast host. That's not who Carl means. The ad
creative practitioner is **Alex Cooper (@alexgoughcooper), co-founder of Adcrate**, a performance
creative agency for DTC brands. He also co-founded the AI consultancy Human² and writes a
Beehiiv newsletter. Motion and CreativeCon materials credit him with 3,000+ ads (1440, Caraway,
Harry's) and $35M+ in managed spend. Those numbers are his own bios, not independently verified.
Sources: [newsletter](https://alexcooper.beehiiv.com/), [X](https://x.com/alexgoughcooper),
[Adcrate](https://adcrate.com/), [CreativeCon 2025 talk](https://www.creativeos.com/creativecon-2025/how-i-make-thousands-of-high-converting-ad-creatives-every-month/),
[D2C Diaries episode](https://www.buzzsprout.com/2307128/episodes/17624085-reinventing-creative-strategy-with-ai-alex-cooper).
I only saw parts of the "7 Rules" post through search snippets (keep it simple, statics have
hooks, design for cold traffic). Read the full post to fill in the other four rules.
